Under Armour

The brand and the brief

Under Armour has owned "Protect This House" for two decades, and for most of that time it has lived where you would expect it to — locker rooms, tunnels, team huddles, sweat on a weight bench. Self-set brief: keep the line, move it out of the locker room, and make it mean something to someone who has never played for a team.

The insight

The house was never the stadium. It is whatever you have decided is yours to defend, and most people are defending something that has no crowd watching. The line is strongest when the stakes are personal rather than athletic — the answer "I will" works better as a vow than as a chant.

The idea

Take "Protect This House" out of the arena and into a heightened, cinematic world where the house is a place worth defending — and let the answer stay the same.

Why it works for Under Armour

It extends the brand's most valuable piece of equity instead of replacing it, which is the cheapest kind of growth a brand can buy. It moves the brand from team sport to personal resolve without softening the tone — still dark, still confrontational, still the brand's own voice. And it scales: the world can hold a product story, an athlete story, or a pure brand film, because the line does the work, not the setting.

The execution

A cinematic spot built in Cinema 4D and composited in After Effects, developing the visual language from two earlier pieces — ToonRun and CityRig 1.6 — into a single heightened tone.
